# Electrochemistry: CHE Step Diagrams and Constant-Potential Concepts

> Covers: computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) thermodynamics, OER/ORR/HER free-energy step diagrams, pH and electrode-potential corrections, overpotential definitions, volcano descriptors, and the conceptual difference between fixed-charge/fixed-electron and constant-potential electrochemical calculations.

## What CHE is for

The computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) is the common Norskov-style thermodynamic model for electrocatalytic screening. It replaces an explicit solvated proton and electron by half of gas-phase H2 at pH = 0 and U = 0 V vs SHE:

```text
mu(H+ + e-) = 1/2 G(H2)
```

For an electrode potential `U` in volts vs SHE and pH at 298.15 K:

```text
mu(H+ + e-) = 1/2 G(H2) - U - 0.0592*pH
```

If using the RHE scale, the pH correction is already included:

```text
U_RHE = U_SHE + 0.0592*pH
mu(H+ + e-) = 1/2 G(H2) - U_RHE
```

Do not apply both an RHE conversion and a separate `0.0592*pH` correction. State the scale every time.

CHE is appropriate for fast thermodynamic step diagrams: OER, ORR, HER, HOR, CO2RR first screenings, NRR first screenings. It does not include transition-state barriers, explicit double-layer structure, solvent dynamics, mass transport, current density, or true potential-dependent electronic structure.

## OER four-step CHE model

Acid-form OER:

```text
* + H2O(l)             -> *OH  + H+ + e-
*OH                    -> *O   + H+ + e-
*O + H2O(l)            -> *OOH + H+ + e-
*OOH                   -> *    + O2(g) + H+ + e-
```

At U = 0 V vs SHE and pH = 0:

```text
DG1 = G(*OH)  + 0.5*G(H2)          - G(*)    - G(H2O)
DG2 = G(*O)   + 0.5*G(H2)          - G(*OH)
DG3 = G(*OOH) + 0.5*G(H2)          - G(*O)   - G(H2O)
DG4 = G(O2)   + G(*) + 0.5*G(H2)   - G(*OOH)
```

The OER total free energy is fixed to 4.92 eV for four electrons, so the ideal step is 1.23 eV. Because DFT O2 is usually problematic, the traditional OER cycle back-calculates O2 from H2O/H2:

```text
2 H2O(l) -> O2(g) + 2 H2(g)     DeltaG = 4.92 eV
G(O2) = 2*G(H2O) - 2*G(H2) + 4.92
```

For plotting cumulative levels:

```text
G0 = G(*) + 2*G(H2O)
G1 = G(*OH)  + G(H2O) + 0.5*G(H2)
G2 = G(*O)   + G(H2O) + G(H2)
G3 = G(*OOH) + 1.5*G(H2)
G4 = G(*) + G(O2) + 2*G(H2)
relative level at U,pH = (Gn - G0) - n*(U + 0.0592*pH), n = 0..4, U vs SHE
```

On the RHE scale, replace `U + 0.0592*pH` with `U_RHE`.

OER limiting potential and overpotential:

```text
DGmax = max(DG1, DG2, DG3, DG4)
U_L,OER = DGmax
eta_OER = DGmax - 1.23
```

The limiting step is the largest uphill step at U = 0 V (or on the RHE-referenced diagram). At U = 1.23 V, any remaining uphill segment equals the thermodynamic overpotential.

## ORR as the reverse of OER

ORR is the reverse reaction:

```text
O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O
```

A consistent ORR diagram can be generated by reversing the OER states and signs. The limiting potential is the highest potential at which all ORR elementary reduction steps remain downhill. In words: OER asks for the largest uphill oxidation step; ORR asks for the smallest downhill reduction margin. Keep one sign convention throughout the analysis.

In alkaline ORR, the written elementary steps often contain OH- rather than H+. Thermodynamically, `H+ + OH- <-> H2O`, so the same CHE machinery can be used after converting references. The pH/electrode scale must still be stated.

## HER two-step picture

Acidic HER is usually summarized by Volmer plus Tafel or Heyrovsky:

```text
Volmer:    * + H+ + e- -> *H
Tafel:     2*H -> 2* + H2
Heyrovsky: *H + H+ + e- -> * + H2
```

The standard CHE descriptor is hydrogen adsorption free energy:

```text
DG_H(U,pH) = G(*H) - G(*) - 0.5*G(H2) + U + 0.0592*pH    # U vs SHE
DG_H(U_RHE) = G(*H) - G(*) - 0.5*G(H2) + U_RHE
```

Good HER catalysts have `DG_H` near 0 eV. Strongly negative means H binds too strongly; strongly positive means H binds too weakly. In alkaline HER, water dissociation in the Volmer step may introduce a kinetic barrier, so a simple `DG_H` descriptor may be incomplete.

## Common free-energy ingredients

A step diagram uses Gibbs-like free energies assembled from electronic energies and corrections:

```text
G = E_DFT + ZPE + DeltaH(T) - T*S(T) + solvation/field/corrections
```

For adsorbates, use adsorbate vibrational corrections when possible; do not apply gas-phase translational/rotational entropy to a bound adsorbate. For gas molecules, use consistent thermochemical corrections at the stated T,p. For water in OER CHE, many workflows use liquid water represented by water vapor at its equilibrium vapor pressure at 298 K with thermochemical correction; state the convention.

Every energy in one CHE expression must use one method fingerprint: functional, dispersion, PAW/basis, cutoff, k-point policy, spin/U policy, solvation model, surface cell, and convergence criteria.

## Volcano descriptors

OER/ORR intermediates often obey approximate scaling relations, for example:

```text
DeltaG_OOH ~= DeltaG_OH + 3.2 eV
DeltaG_O   ~= 2*DeltaG_OH
```

These relations make volcano plots possible. For OER, the activity descriptor is controlled by the largest of the four step energies; for ORR, by the most restrictive downhill reduction step. The ideal OER point is roughly `DeltaG_OH = 1.23 eV`, `DeltaG_O = 2.46 eV`, `DeltaG_OOH = 3.69 eV`, giving zero thermodynamic overpotential. Real catalysts are limited by deviations from these ideal spacings.

## Constant-potential concepts

A conventional DFT calculation fixes the number of electrons. An electrode experiment is closer to fixed electrode potential: the electrode exchanges charge with an electron reservoir to maintain a target electron chemical potential. Constant-potential DFT is therefore grand-canonical in spirit.

Conceptual relation:

```text
Omega = F - q*mu_e
```

where `F` is a fixed-electron free energy, `q` is the solute/electrode charge under the implementation's sign convention, and `mu_e` is the electron chemical potential. Different codes define signs and references differently, so read the implementation documentation before comparing numbers.

Why constant potential matters:

- adsorbate energies can depend on electrode charge and field;
- barriers can shift with potential;
- charged intermediates and electrolyte screening require a reference to the solution potential;
- fixed-charge calculations may correspond to different potentials for different adsorbate states.

Why CHE is still often enough:

- for simple OER/ORR/HER screening, CHE step diagrams are cheap, transparent, and widely comparable;
- the largest uncertainty is often the DFT functional, adsorbate scaling relation, surface model, or solvation correction rather than constant-potential response;
- constant-potential models require extra calibration and can introduce new implementation-specific conventions.

Use constant-potential calculations when the scientific question depends on charge, field, double layer, explicit potential-dependent barriers, or charged intermediates. Use CHE for ordinary thermodynamic screening unless there is a reason to go beyond it.

## Fixed-charge potential scans with implicit electrolyte

Many VASPsol electrochemical papers use a practical fixed-charge scan rather than a self-consistent fixed-potential run. The calculation changes `NELECT`, the implicit electrolyte supplies counter-charge, and each charge state corresponds to a different electrode potential. Energies are then fitted as a function of potential and compared at the same target `U`.

Typical potential conversion for one common VASPsol + `FERMI_SHIFT` convention:

```text
U_vs_SHE = -(Efermi + FERMI_SHIFT) - 4.44
```

Here `Efermi` is read from `OUTCAR`, `FERMI_SHIFT` from the VASP stdout file, and `4.44 eV` is the common absolute SHE reference used by that workflow. Other functionals/solvation models may use a different calibrated SHE value.

The charged fixed-electron energy is commonly corrected before fitting:

```text
q = NELECT - NELECT_PZC
E_corr = E_VASP + q*FERMI_SHIFT - q*(Efermi + FERMI_SHIFT)
```

Keep the sign convention with the spreadsheet or script that generated the analysis. Under this convention the expression is algebraically equivalent to `E_VASP - q*Efermi`, but the unsimplified form is easier to audit because it exposes the two quantities extracted from VASP output.

Fit the corrected energies to a capacitor-like parabola:

```text
E_corr(U) = a*U^2 + b*U + c
```

or equivalently:

```text
E_corr(U) = 0.5*C*(U - U_PZC)^2 + E_PZC
```

The potential of zero charge is the neutral-charge potential in a direct scan, or the minimum of the fitted parabola when using the capacitor form. Curvature gives an effective capacitance under the chosen model. Adsorption energies at constant potential should be obtained by evaluating two fitted curves at the same `U` and then subtracting, not by subtracting raw fixed-charge points that sit at different potentials.

This workflow is related to homogeneous-background and Poisson-Boltzmann implicit-electrolyte approaches: the charged slab is stabilized by a compensating continuum charge rather than by explicit ions. It is not the same as a VASPsol++ `EFERMI_ref` calculation, where the calculation directly targets an electron chemical potential and reports a grand-potential-like quantity.

## Reporting checklist

- Reaction, elementary steps, and sign convention.
- Potential scale: SHE or RHE; pH; temperature.
- Reference energies for `H2`, `H2O`, and any back-calculated species such as `O2`.
- Free-energy corrections included and omitted.
- Step energies, limiting step, limiting potential, and overpotential.
- Whether the model is vacuum CHE, implicit solvent, explicit solvent, or constant potential.
- For fixed-charge potential scans, `NELECT_PZC`, charge convention, `Efermi`, `FERMI_SHIFT`, SHE calibration, corrected-energy formula, fitted potential range, and fit coefficients.
- For constant-potential work, electron chemical potential reference, calibration to SHE/RHE, electrolyte model, and whether reported values are free energies or grand potentials.
